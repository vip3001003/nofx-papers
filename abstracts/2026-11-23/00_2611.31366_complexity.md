# Locality from Nonlocality: Symmetry Relocalization and Gauging of Lattice Dirac Fermions

- **arXiv**: [2611.31366](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31366)
- **Date**: 2026-11-23
- **Category**: complexity
- **Authors**: Lei Su
- **NOFX Relevance Score**: 0

## Abstract

Spatially nonlocal symmetries need not imply nonlocal gauge dynamics. Nonlocal canonical transformations make certain exact nonlocal compact symmetries of free lattice Dirac fermions onsite while retaining sufficient Hamiltonian locality for gauging. Symmetry relocalization yields exact or emergent gauge locality in 1D, 2D, and 3D, subject to lattice structure, topology, and anomalies. In 1D, a Hamiltonian-preserving transformation exchanges onsite vector and nonlocal spectral axial charges of a half-filled chain, enabling local gauging of each and exposing their mixed anomaly. Bosonization identifies momentum-winding T-duality, with a parity-projected operator flowing to a noninvertible topological defect. A nonperturbative norm-resolvent bound proves emergent dynamical gauge locality at finite chemical potential, while a doubled Chern-insulator parent reveals the duality's bulk origin and superconducting extensions reveal hidden compact symmetries. In 2D, the square $π$-flux Majorana model shares the Kitaev honeycomb model's single Dirac fermion and odd Pfaffian parity but supports sharp and exponentially local compact charges, the latter with nontrivial Chern topology. Relocalization retains Schwartz locality for asymptotically local gauging, while AIII/AII parents clarify bulk charge origin and parity-anomaly inflow. Two flavors yield compact $U(2)$, a quaternionic invariant, and asymptotically local non-Abelian gauging. In 3D, an exact mixed vector-axial flux response is derived, while a distinct model supports an exponentially local vector $U(1)$ charge with strong quaternionic topology. A nonlocal map makes the charge and Kramers symmetry onsite while preserving exponential Hamiltonian locality. For a compact $SU(2)$ Weyl doublet, a no-go theorem forbids translation-preserving Gaussian onsite relocalization retaining Weyl content and Hamiltonian locality.
