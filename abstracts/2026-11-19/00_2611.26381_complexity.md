# Imaginary-Time Density Correlations Encode Quantum Entropy and Free Energy

- **arXiv**: [2611.26381](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26381)
- **Date**: 2026-11-19
- **Category**: complexity
- **Authors**: Sagi Meir
- **NOFX Relevance Score**: 0

## Abstract

In the classical limit, maximum-entropy inference yields an estimate of the excess entropy from the static structure factor through a single momentum sum. Quantum mechanically this estimate fails, because interactions also change the kinetic energy, while delocalization and exchange add information that equal-time correlations do not contain. We show that imaginary-time density correlations contain this information, and that with them the classical relation carries over to quantum systems. Such correlations are available from path-integral simulations and X-ray Thomson scattering. Relative-entropy inference over imaginary-time paths, with an ideal Bose or Fermi gas as reference, gives closed forms for the excess entropy and free energies, within the same Gaussian approximation as the classical relation. They recover the classical result and estimate entropy that the classical relation misses. We test them on weakly interacting bosons, interacting fermions on a ring, and the warm dense electron gas. The closed forms need only imaginary-time density correlations and their ideal-gas reference at a single state point, without using the interaction potential. Our approach can extend other classical entropy estimators to quantum systems.
