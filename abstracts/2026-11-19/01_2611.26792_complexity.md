# Infection Spreading and Stochastic Extinction in Finite Spatially Inhomogeneous Susceptible-Infected-Susceptible and Susceptible-Infected-Recovered Systems

- **arXiv**: [2611.26792](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26792)
- **Date**: 2026-11-19
- **Category**: complexity
- **Authors**: Sara Shabani, Uwe C. Täuber
- **NOFX Relevance Score**: 1

## Abstract

Demographic fluctuations can drive finite epidemic populations into an absorbing disease-free state even when the corresponding infinite system supports sustained activity. By means of Monte Carlo simulations, we investigate how spatial coupling of two distinct subsystems modifies stochastic extinction and epidemic spreading in susceptible-infectious-susceptible (SIS) and susceptible-infectious-recovered (SIR) models on inhomogeneous two-dimensional square lattices. For isolated SIS systems, extinction times exhibit approximate power-law scaling near the epidemic threshold even for small systems. Traversal times characterize the distinct propagation dynamics of SIS and SIR infection clusters. We consider diffusively coupled regions with different transmission characteristics in coupled SIS-SIS, SIR-SIS, and SIR-SIR configurations. Invading fronts can reactivate a vulnerable SIS region after its initial infection has disappeared and establish a long-lived active epidemic state. This stabilization can be induced by either a sustained SIS source or a single transient SIR epidemic wave. Spatial correlation measurements support a mechanism in which the invading activity generates an extended correlated infectious population, effectively providing favorable initial conditions for renewed epidemic spreading. Correlations develop particularly strongly parallel to the subsystem interfaces. Decoupling the regions after some elapsed period demonstrates that autonomous persistence requires sufficient coupling time beyond the first interface arrival for this correlated population to become established. Increasing system size delays invasion but enhances the probability of ultimate successful stabilization. In two coupled SIR regions, transmission can initiate a macroscopic secondary outbreak, while permanent immunity restricts both epidemics to transient activity.
