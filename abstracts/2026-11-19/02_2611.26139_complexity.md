# Local-channel inequivalence within analytic symmetry-broken phases

- **arXiv**: [2611.26139](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26139)
- **Date**: 2026-11-19
- **Category**: complexity
- **Authors**: Guanxi Xun, Rintaro Masaoka, Weiguang Cao et al.
- **NOFX Relevance Score**: 2

## Abstract

Gibbs states at different temperatures can be inequivalent under two-way quasi-local channels even within a single phase with spontaneously broken continuous symmetry and analytic free energy. We prove this for the symmetry-invariant Gibbs states of a canonical ideal Bose gas at fixed density and a classical vortex-free Villain model on hypercubic lattices in $d\ge3$ dimensions. At lower temperatures, two distant observers can estimate the shared direction of order more accurately. The maximum mutual information they can obtain by separate measurements grows logarithmically with the radius of their regions. The leading term is the same throughout each ordered phase, while the constant term $\mathcal{C}(T)=\tfrac12\ln[Υ(T)/T]+c_{\mathrm{geom}}$ is strictly larger at lower temperatures. Here $Υ(T)$ is the phase stiffness and $c_{\mathrm{geom}}$ depends only on the region geometry. A quasi-local channel cannot increase this constant, so it is invariant under two-way conversion. Cooling would increase it and is therefore impossible between any two distinct temperatures in the ordered phase. On finite lattices, cooling with vanishing error requires a circuit whose causal radius grows at least linearly with the lattice side length. We also obtain the invariant for quantum and multicomponent Villain models and conjecture a stiffness formula for more general patterns of continuous symmetry breaking.
