# Exact finite-time fluctuation-response relations for Markov processes: A martingale framework

- **arXiv**: [2611.26267](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26267)
- **Date**: 2026-11-19
- **Category**: complexity
- **Authors**: Tan Van Vu, Van Tuan Vo, Ruicheng Bao et al.
- **NOFX Relevance Score**: 1

## Abstract

Fluctuations and responses of time-integrated observables are both experimentally measurable, and relations between them can reveal the dissipation that sustains a nonequilibrium state. Here we develop a martingale framework that gives exact finite-time fluctuation-response identities for additive observables of general Markov processes. We obtain an exact identity for the variance of an observable in terms of its Doob martingale, which splits the total variance into a part inherited from the initial ensemble and a part generated by noise during the observation. We show that, at fixed preparation, the response of the mean of the observable to any admissible perturbation is the covariance between the Doob martingale and a second martingale that represents the perturbation. For diffusions and jump processes, a single martingale amplitude determines both the fluctuations and the response of the observable. These identities yield inequalities, variational principles, and multivariate and frequency-domain bounds, whose costs are set by the corresponding physical perturbations. Applying the framework to underdamped dynamics, we obtain a friction-response thermodynamic uncertainty relation (TUR) that can be saturated at any finite time and thus gives a variational principle for the entropy production. We also demonstrate exponentially strong violations of the conventional TUR by velocity-resolved currents, even in linear Gaussian dynamics, and derive a weak-noise dissipation-coherence bound for limit cycles, which shows that inertial oscillators can beat the overdamped bound only if their frequency is weakly sensitive to friction. For overdamped dynamics and jump processes, the framework recovers and extends recent fluctuation-response identities and identifies when the corresponding uncertainty relations are saturated.
