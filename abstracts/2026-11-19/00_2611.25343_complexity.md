# Semiclassical Averaging and Temporal Fluctuations of Quantum Dark Solitons in the Lieb-Liniger Model

- **arXiv**: [2611.25343](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25343)
- **Date**: 2026-11-19
- **Category**: complexity
- **Authors**: Kota Kaneko, Kohei Motegi, Jun Sato
- **NOFX Relevance Score**: 0

## Abstract

We study the time evolution of quantum dark-soliton states of the Lieb--Liniger model, introduced by Sato--Kanamoto--Kaminishi--Deguchi as equal-weight superpositions of one-hole yrast states, and their relation to Gross--Pitaevskii (GP) solitons. We consider a simultaneous weak-coupling and large-system limit in which the repulsive coupling constant $c$ tends to zero and the particle number $N$ and ring circumference $L$ tend to infinity, with the mean density $\bar n=N/L$ and scaled circumference $Λ=L\sqrt{c\bar n}$ fixed. We conjecture that, in this limit, the normalized quantum density and its evolution at fixed scaled time $τ=c\bar n t$ are described by a canonical-momentum average of normalized periodic one-soliton GP densities, each evolving at its own velocity. We explicitly parametrize the GP family by canonical momentum and provide numerical evidence for this correspondence. The different velocities explain the spreading of the averaged density notch despite shape preservation by individual solitons. In the infinite-line limit, we derive an analytic expression for the initial averaged profile, establish an inverse-fifth-power decay of its density deficit with distance from the soliton center, and express the Fourier transform of its deviation from the background as a series of Bickley--Naylor functions $\operatorname{Ki}_3$. Following the spreading of the initial notch, we investigate temporal density fluctuations of the quantum dark solitons and propose a relation between long-time mode variances and second moments of GP Fourier coefficients, supported by numerical comparisons of individual mode variances and the total variance. Yrast transition frequencies distinguish the initial dephasing time scale from the longer scale associated with finite-time averaging of fluctuations.
