# Simple Routes to Turing-Like Patterns

- **arXiv**: [2611.26814](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26814)
- **Date**: 2026-11-19
- **Category**: complexity
- **Authors**: Zhan Zhao, Xiang-Ning Li, Wen-Wen Li et al.
- **NOFX Relevance Score**: 0

## Abstract

Spots, stripes, and networks recur in systems with very different microscopic dynamics, suggesting that the competing processes behind Turing-like patterns may be easy to realise. We ask whether local amplification coupled to suppression over a wider region can generate these geometries in simple systems beyond chemical reaction--diffusion models. We test this idea in two settings, a binary cellular automaton obtained by adding a distant neighbour count to Conway's Game of Life and a self-gravitating toy universe described by particle motion and two continuous fields. The automaton produces spots, stripes, and coexistence across its control plane, while a rule retaining only the two opposing counts produces islands, stripes, and holes. Directional correlations and intrinsic dimension identify the same region of orientational order. In the gravitational system, convergence increases local density while mass transport depletes the surroundings. The two-field and particle densities have a smoothed spatial correlation of $0.962$ at scale factor $a=1$ under the original force law, and strengthening its long-wavelength component drives both descriptions toward fewer dense condensations. Together, these results show how simple counting rules and redistribution of a conserved quantity can realise competing growth and suppression, providing two concrete routes by which Turing-like geometries can recur across different dynamics.
