# Dynamical Variational Theory of Stochastic Reaction Networks

- **arXiv**: [2611.25491](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25491)
- **Date**: 2026-11-19
- **Category**: complexity
- **Authors**: Moshir Harsh, Peter Sollich
- **NOFX Relevance Score**: 0

## Abstract

Discrete stochastic population dynamics describe systems ranging from biochemical reaction networks and gene regulation to epidemic spreading, ecology, and population genetics, where finite populations generate strong intrinsic fluctuations. Predicting their dynamics, including mean populations, fluctuations, time-lagged correlations, response functions, and steady states, remains challenging: the chemical master equation admits no general analytical solution, numerical solutions scale poorly with system size, and standard approximations fail at low copy numbers. Using a field-theoretic representation of the reaction dynamics, we develop a dynamical variational approximation for stochastic reaction networks based on the Plefka expansion from spin-glass physics. For reaction systems with general stoichiometry and time-dependent rates, the expansion yields tractable non-Markovian effective dynamics with self-consistent memory. We further introduce a generalized self-consistent bubble resummation that provides closed predictions for means, correlations, and response functions. The formulation applies broadly to discrete stochastic population dynamics on networks, and we provide a numerical implementation with adaptive time stepping for user-defined systems. We benchmark the method on stochastic gene regulation, enzyme kinetics, and infection dynamics, where it accurately captures both time-dependent behaviour and steady states, including fluctuation-driven effects missed by conventional approximations. Together, these results establish a general analytical framework for stochastic reaction dynamics across transient and stationary regimes in strongly fluctuating low-copy-number systems.
