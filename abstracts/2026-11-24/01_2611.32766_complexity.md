# Ordering Information in Multispecies Long-Range Swap Processes

- **arXiv**: [2611.32766](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32766)
- **Date**: 2026-11-24
- **Category**: complexity
- **Authors**: Eunghyun Lee
- **NOFX Relevance Score**: 1

## Abstract

We measure how much the left-to-right species order $W_t$ of interacting particles on the integer lattice reveals about their initial configuration. For distinct species under long-range swaps, deleting stronger particles gives coordinates with independent Poisson increments. For a finitely supported initial law, the mutual information between any function $U$ of the initial configuration and $W_t$ is $C_U/t+O(t^{-3/2})$ as $t\to\infty$, where $C_U$, which may vanish, is determined by the means of Gaussian order statistics and the conditional means of the initial compressed coordinates given $U$. On consecutive sites with a uniform initial order, we compute the coefficient of the $t\log(1/t)$ term in the short-time information loss for arbitrary species counts. For three particles in this setting, repeating the weaker rather than the stronger species triples the long-time $t^{-1}$ coefficient, although both compositions have the same composition entropy and initial-order entropy. These long-time coefficients are the same whether equal particles block or push each other, but the nonuniform limiting output law is reversed. In the ordinary multispecies totally asymmetric simple exclusion process (TASEP), two distinct particles at fixed sites with a uniform initial order instead give $t^{-1/2}$ decay.
