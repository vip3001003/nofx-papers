# Quantifying combined rate- and noise-induced transitions in a multiscale system

- **arXiv**: [2611.33449](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33449)
- **Date**: 2026-11-24
- **Category**: complexity
- **Authors**: Ryan Deeley, Peter Ashwin, Ulrike Feudel
- **NOFX Relevance Score**: 2

## Abstract

Excitations in multiscale dynamical systems produce persistent but impermanent excursions away from a quiescent state. A novel mechanism for inducing excitations, through a combination of rate-dependent parametric and stochastic forcing, is discussed using the FitzHugh-Nagumo model as an example. In the absence of noise, a quasithreshold separating the state space between supercritical and subcritical behaviour is defined with respect to slow manifolds associated with the metastable excited state. This is formulated in an autonomous system framework, which can be obtained via a compactification of time. Techniques from singular perturbation theory elucidate how canard trajectories shape the geometric structure of the quasithreshold as the system's pullback attractor moves through it. The probability with which weakly stochastically driven sample paths travel through the quasithreshold and undertake (i) solely noise-induced and (ii) combined rate- and noise-induced excitations is estimated numerically for each of the corresponding forcing scenarios, using the adaptive multilevel splitting algorithm. The probability of weak stochastic forcing preventing a rate-induced excitation, which occurs deterministically, is likewise estimated. This quantifies the different rates at which the probability of each transition scenario scales exponentially with the noise strength, in accordance with sample path large deviation principles. It is revealed that a combination of (deterministically subcritical) parametric and stochastic forcing is exponentially more likely to induce excitations than stochastic forcing alone, which is attributed to the pullback attractor and quasithreshold converging towards one another.
