# Annihilation versus anchoring of a topologically charged pair on a perforated two-dimensional surface

- **arXiv**: [2611.17570](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17570)
- **Date**: 2026-11-12
- **Category**: complexity
- **Authors**: Patrick Romanescu
- **NOFX Relevance Score**: 0

## Abstract

Two independent random walks of opposite topological charge move on the discrete torus $(\mathbb{Z}/n\mathbb{Z})^2$, which contains $k$ well-separated holes. The walks annihilate when they come within a core distance of each other, and each walk is anchored when it first touches a hole. We prove that the probability of annihilation before anchoring converges to $w_0/(w_0+\sum_j w_j)$, where the weights are explicit and depend only logarithmically on the sizes of the core and of the holes; for point-like core and holes, the limit is $1/(1+k)$. We then prove a fate theorem: the pair either terminates or ends as two opposite charges anchored on two different holes, with probability $(k-1)/(k+1)$ in the point-like case, and the hosting pair $\{j,l\}$ is chosen with probability proportional to $w_jw_l$. The proof combines an abstract competition lemma for rare sets in reversible Markov chains with sharp two-dimensional Green's function estimates.
