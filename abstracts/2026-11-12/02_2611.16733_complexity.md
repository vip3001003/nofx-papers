# Freezing transition and shock-like transients in ballistic random walks under threshold resetting

- **arXiv**: [2611.16733](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16733)
- **Date**: 2026-11-12
- **Category**: complexity
- **Authors**: Rahul Das, Satya N Majumdar, Arnab Pal
- **NOFX Relevance Score**: 2

## Abstract

We investigate the transient dynamics of a one-dimensional ballistic random walker under threshold resetting (TR). The walker starts at $x_0>0$, moves towards a threshold at the origin with a velocity drawn from a distribution $φ(v)$, and on reaching the threshold is reset to $x_0$ with a new sampled velocity. Whenever the mean first-passage time to the threshold is finite, the walker reaches a steady state that is uniform on $[0,x_0]$ and independent of $φ(v)$. The relaxation towards it, in contrast, is highly sensitive to the behavior of $φ(v)$. For a large class of $φ(v)$-s, we find, rather remarkably, that the relaxation rate depends explicitly on the spatial coordinate, in contrast to conventional relaxation phenomena, where the rate is typically position-independent. Furthermore, this space-dependent relaxation rate eventually freezes to a constant, position-independent value beyond a critical position, a phenomenon we identify as \textit{freezing transition}. Additionally, for a power-law velocity distribution, the system exhibits an anomalously slow, algebraic relaxation towards the steady state. We also showed that a sharp cutoff in $φ(v)$ generates a shock-like discontinuity in the particle propagator that travels towards the threshold, with a jump height that decays with time. These results demonstrate how velocity fluctuations, despite leaving the steady state unchanged, can profoundly influence the transient spatial structures and showcase rich non-equilibrium behavior. Our analytical results are corroborated by Monte Carlo simulations, showing an excellent agreement.
