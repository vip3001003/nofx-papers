# Elasto-inertial force exerted on a sphere oscillating in a fluid near a soft interface

- **arXiv**: [2611.16575](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16575)
- **Date**: 2026-11-12
- **Category**: complexity
- **Authors**: Axel Batalha, Harshit Joshi, Yacine Amarouchene et al.
- **NOFX Relevance Score**: 0

## Abstract

Fluid inertia can remain important in confined flows, even at low Reynolds number when convective inertia is negligible; yet its coupling with interface deformability is an open problem. Here, we theoretically investigate the high-frequency oscillations of an immersed sphere near an elastic wall in the lubrication regime. We first reconcile previous predictions for the added mass near a rigid wall by consistently matching the inner lubrication flow with the outer flow. Using this rigid-wall problem as a reference, we then develop a perturbative analysis of wall deformation and derive the leading elasto-inertial correction to the hydrodynamic force. Our results reveal that the coupling between interface softness and fluid inertia produces a dominant non-dissipative force contribution scaling as the power four of the frequency in the high-frequency limit, thus corresponding to the fourth time derivative of the sphere's position. This finding may have implications regarding the short-time dynamics of colloidal particles near deformable interfaces in biological and soft-matter settings.
