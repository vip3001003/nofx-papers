# Work extraction from a single negative-temperature bath: a temperature gradient-driven heat flow

- **arXiv**: [2611.16743](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16743)
- **Date**: 2026-11-12
- **Category**: complexity
- **Authors**: Wolfgang Rudolf Bauer
- **NOFX Relevance Score**: 0

## Abstract

Work dissipation into a thermal bath is standard, whereas the reverse process -- sole work extraction from a thermal bath without a net state change -- contradicts the Kelvin-Planck formulation of the second law of thermodynamics. However, this prohibition is bypassed for baths at negative absolute temperature, sparking ongoing debate regarding their thermodynamic stability. Here, we reconcile the second law with sole work extraction from a negative-temperature bath by considering a large virtual assembly of weakly coupled working systems rather than an isolated machine. By assigning this assembly a canonical temperature derived from its Shannon/information entropy, the framework naturally extends to molecular-scale machines. We demonstrate paradigmatic examples where the assembly's canonical temperature is cooler than the negative-temperature bath. Consequently, sole work extraction represents standard heat flow from a hot system (the negative-temperature bath) to a cooler system (the working assembly), rendering the process fully consistent with the second law. This informational and statistical-mechanical framework also naturally explains the counter-intuitive phenomenon of "donkey behavior", where increasing the workload in a negative-temperature bath leads to enhanced work extraction.
