# Tracing dependency networks explains how origins shape downstream vulnerability

- **arXiv**: [2611.16326](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16326)
- **Date**: 2026-11-12
- **Category**: network
- **Authors**: Ruiwu Niu, Muhammad Azhar, Michaël Antonie van Wyk et al.
- **NOFX Relevance Score**: 1

## Abstract

Many systems sustain outcomes through chains of prerequisites, yet these dependencies are often partially observed. We trace backward from sampled outcomes to recover dependency structure and measure edge importance by the fraction of outcomes lost after an edge fails. Across 1,620 synthetic networks, revealing 90% of origins required tracing 7.24% of outcomes on average. We define pair thickness as the number of origin and outcome pairs connected through an edge. Under strict dependency, where every input is necessary, thickness counts each affected outcome once per upstream origin, whereas loss counts it once. Edges therefore form origin groups with distinct thickness and loss relations, also seen in software, citation and river topologies. Within a network, greater origin diversity corresponds to lower strict loss at equal complete thickness. We develop a growth model that predicts how edges populate these groups and how losses are distributed under partial observation, tested on 2,560 independent networks. Separating origin counts from downstream reach improves identification of edges causing the greatest losses among unsampled outcomes in strict synthetic tests. Substitutable inputs reduce losses and change which importance scores perform best. Edge importance thus measures a dependency's contribution to sustaining later outcomes, guiding the protection of critical connections.
