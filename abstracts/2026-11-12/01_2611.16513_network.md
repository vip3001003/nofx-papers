# Scale-rich network metamaterials grow by local rules on a recursive cell division

- **arXiv**: [2611.16513](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16513)
- **Date**: 2026-11-12
- **Category**: network
- **Authors**: Alexei Vazquez
- **NOFX Relevance Score**: 1

## Abstract

Scale-rich (SR) metamaterials are planar ligament networks grown by nucleating a segment at a random point and angle and extending it until it meets the existing structure. In the dual graph, where ligaments are nodes and junctions are links, the degree distribution has a $k^{-3}$ tail. We show that the SR construction is Cowan's iterated cell division with area-weighted selection, so the cell genealogy is a random binary search tree, and that the dual grows by a face-local rule: a new node joins two nodes on the boundary of one face. Preferential attachment on face incidences would give the Barabási--Albert law, but the geometry works differently. A ligament is hit in proportion to its length, fixed at birth, and its degree is close to a Poisson variable with mean $4\ell_i(\sqrt T-\sqrt i)/c_L$, without reinforcement. Length is a fitness assigned by the cell hierarchy, $\ell_i\propto i^{-1/2}$, and the $k^{-3}$ tail follows. Integral geometry gives the probability that a new ligament cuts off a corner of its cell, which sets the triangle closure and the clustering spectrum $C(k)\simeq2p/k$. Square-closing splits raise the shortest-path multiplicity above its value at fixed degree sequence. Thickness, jamming, angle sets and density gradients leave $γ=3$; coherent orientation fields give $γ\simeq2.4$--$2.5$. For plates in three dimensions the fitness is the birth area and $γ=5/2$, whereas preferential attachment would give $2.44$; fluctuation tests select the fitness mechanism. The reported per-ligament energy law cannot distinguish degree from length or age.
