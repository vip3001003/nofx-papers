# Where are the shortest-path nodes in spatial networks?

- **arXiv**: [2611.14152](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14152)
- **Date**: 2026-11-10
- **Category**: network
- **Authors**: Xinhan Liu, Robert Kooij, Piet Van Mieghem
- **NOFX Relevance Score**: 0

## Abstract

To predict the spatial distribution of shortest-path nodes, we propose a Gaussian estimate of the shortest-path node probability $π_{uv}(w)$, the probability that a node $w$ lies on a shortest path between a source $u$ and a destination $v$. According to the Gaussian estimate, three variables affect the shortest-path node probability $π_{uv}(w)$ and combine into one variable, the standardized detour $X_{uvw}$. In our simulations of hard and soft random geometric graphs in Euclidean, hyperbolic and spherical space, the standardized detour $X_{uvw}$ alone approximately determines how fast the shortest-path node probability $π_{uv}(w)$ decreases as the node $w$ moves away from the geodesic between the source $u$ and the destination $v$. We find and explain that, when the hopcount variance $σ^2(H_{uv})$ is very small, the shortest-path nodes have a discrete spatial distribution, a quantized distribution of shortest-path nodes. We further extend the study to spatially inhomogeneous networks, for which we propose a method to find the shortest-path nodes.
