# Dynamics of Rényi entanglement entropies and Eigenstate Thermalization

- **arXiv**: [2611.13141](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13141)
- **Date**: 2026-11-09
- **Category**: complexity
- **Authors**: Riccardo Senese, Pasquale Calabrese
- **NOFX Relevance Score**: 1

## Abstract

How is entanglement dynamics encoded in the eigenstates of the time-evolution operator? We address this question by focusing on Rényi entropy dynamics in deterministic chaotic Floquet models in arbitrary dimensions. Inspired by the eigenstate thermalization hypothesis (ETH), we employ spectral representations of reduced density matrix moments (purity and its higher-order generalizations) whose decay through spectral dephasing captures nonequilibrium entanglement growth. These representations closely resemble those governing local-observable dynamics, but involve eigenstates in a replicated Hilbert space and observables are replaced by subsystem permutation operators, such as the swap operator for Rényi-2. Focusing on Rényi-2, we combine exact random matrix theory calculations, established ETH results and numerical tests in 1D to formulate ETH-like ansätze for subsystem swap matrix elements in the replicated eigenbasis and their cross-correlations with initial-state overlap statistics. In the thermodynamic limit, purity dynamics is entirely determined by smooth slowly varying functions that emerge from such statistics, paralleling generalized ETH formulations in a single replica. In the limit of large subsystems, these functions exhibit a striking structural simplification: locality of the dynamics together with bulk spectral dephasing reduce their functional dependence to a single fundamental frequency. Our formalism provides a setting where ETH-like statements naturally coexist for both arbitrarily small and large subsystem sizes. We discuss extensions of our results to higher Rényi entropies. Beyond clarifying the spectral foundations of entanglement dynamics, these results lay some of the groundwork for future applications of spectral filter methods to the computation of Rényi evolution in deterministic systems.
