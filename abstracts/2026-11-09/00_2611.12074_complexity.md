# Linear response in generalized Lotka--Volterra systems with a finite number of species

- **arXiv**: [2611.12074](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12074)
- **Date**: 2026-11-09
- **Category**: complexity
- **Authors**: Koya Katayama, Sosuke Ito
- **NOFX Relevance Score**: 0

## Abstract

Understanding how species interactions shape ecosystem responses to perturbations is a central challenge in ecology. Random matrix theory (RMT) has been widely used to predict the responses of large random ecosystems, but its predictions may not accurately describe individual finite ecosystems. Here, we derive rigorous geometric bounds on the numerical ranges and spectra of Jacobian matrices that are valid for any finite number of species without assuming randomness. These bounds link long-term stability and short-term stability to the symmetric component of abundance-weighted interactions, and constrain the maximum oscillation frequency by the skew-symmetric component. Applied to random ensembles, the bounds also yield constraints on ensemble averages of long-term stability, short-term stability, and the maximum oscillation frequency in terms of standard RMT parameters. Using empirical data from marine food webs, we show that our bounds provide informative constraints on short-term stability and the maximum oscillation frequency even where RMT-based predictions fail. These results establish structural constraints on local ecosystem responses that complement RMT-based statistical predictions for large random ecosystems.
