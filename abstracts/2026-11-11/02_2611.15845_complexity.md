# Potts Order Preempts Emergence of 2-Form U(1) Spin Liquidity in Three-Dimensional Spin Vorticity Models

- **arXiv**: [2611.15845](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15845)
- **Date**: 2026-11-11
- **Category**: complexity
- **Authors**: Michael D. Burke, Mohid U. Farooqi, Kristian Tyn Kai Chung et al.
- **NOFX Relevance Score**: 2

## Abstract

Three-dimensional (3D) spin vorticity models (SVMs) have been proposed to realize an exotic 2-form U(1) classical spin liquid (CSL), characterized by a zero-curl constraint, string excitations, and deconfined fluctuating membranes. A previous numerical study [PRB 111, 064417 (2025)] uncovered a puzzle in the pyrochlore lattice SVM: despite exhibiting pinch points characteristic of a U(1) spin liquid, it also develops weak all-in--all-out (AIAO) order. Here, we show that the emergence of the 2-form CSL in 3D SVMs is preempted by an intrinsic instability towards entropy-driven height locking. We establish this by resolving the ground state constraints using a height mapping, which we further relate to a 3-state Potts antiferromagnet. This identifies the pyrochlore's weak AIAO order as permutationally symmetric sublattice (PSS) order, albeit with an exceptionally weak locking strength. We extend the SVM to lattices of corner-sharing octahedra and cubes, where we identify the alternative broken-sublattice-symmetry (BSS) order, a hidden order without magnetic Bragg peaks coexisting with collective paramagnetism. All three models undergo a 3D Ising transition consistent with a symmetry-allowed locking potential in the coarse-grained height theory. The spin liquid persists over intermediate length and temperature scales, but a critical Coulomb ground state ensemble fails to materialize, its correlations cut off by the finite locking length. These findings illustrate how microscopic constraints can determine the ultimate stability of emergent gauge theories in frustrated spin systems.
