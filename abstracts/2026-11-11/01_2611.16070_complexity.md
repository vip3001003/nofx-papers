# Entropy production decomposition and fluctuation theorems under continuous feedback: From prescribed to stochastic protocols

- **arXiv**: [2611.16070](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16070)
- **Date**: 2026-11-11
- **Category**: complexity
- **Authors**: Long Him Cheung
- **NOFX Relevance Score**: 1

## Abstract

When a system is driven by a prescribed protocol, the entropy production can be decomposed into the housekeeping and non-autonomous contributions, each satisfying a fluctuation theorem. Under feedback control, however, the feedback parameter depends on measurement outcomes rather than following a prescribed protocol. To study how the decomposition changes, we consider an overdamped Brownian particle at position $x$ with a low-pass-filtered measurement signal $D$ as the feedback parameter. Regarding each realization of $D$ as a stochastic protocol, we derive fluctuation theorems for the entropy production associated with the dynamics of $x$ and of $D$, and interpret them in terms of the information flow. For a solvable harmonic model, we map the dynamics onto an autonomous system in which the measurement and filtering process acts as an effective thermal bath. Feedback cooling thus corresponds to heat conduction from the physical bath through the particle to this colder bath, and the information flow can be interpreted as the entropy associated with the heat conduction between $x$ and $D$. We verify these results by numerical simulations.
