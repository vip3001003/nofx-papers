# Eigenvalue Crossings and Discontinuous BBP Transitions in Spiked Euclidean Random Matrices

- **arXiv**: [2611.15927](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.15927)
- **Date**: 2026-11-11
- **Category**: complexity
- **Authors**: Pasquale Casaburi, Pierpaolo Vivo
- **NOFX Relevance Score**: 1

## Abstract

We study the recovery of a rank-one signal embedded in a symmetric Euclidean random matrix, whose dense and geometrically correlated entries generally lack rotational invariance. We show that signal recovery through the leading eigenvector is governed by a competition between the principal directions of the noise and the signal. In particular, the signal may already be encoded in a subleading eigendirection, while remaining undetected by the leading principal component. As the signal strength increases, a crossing between competing eigenvalues can change their ordering, causing the leading eigenvector to switch abruptly from a noise-dominated to a signal-bearing direction. This mechanism gives rise to a discontinuous BBP-type transition, in which the overlap with the signal jumps from zero to a finite value. Using the replica method, we reduce the problem to a Fredholm eigenvalue equation and derive general criteria for the existence of a positive recovery threshold, expressed in terms of the unperturbed spectral edge, the spike mean and the spatial averages of the principal eigenfunctions. We obtain analytically the limiting largest eigenvalue, eigenvector-component distribution and signal overlap, and illustrate the results with explicit solutions for quadratic distance and rank-two kernels, finding excellent agreement with numerical diagonalisation.
