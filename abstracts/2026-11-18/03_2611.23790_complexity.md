# Finite-time stochastic resetting in bounded domains: competing targets, energetic cost, and optimal search strategies

- **arXiv**: [2611.23790](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23790)
- **Date**: 2026-11-18
- **Category**: complexity
- **Authors**: Hanshuang Chen, Chongyao Tang, Guofeng Li
- **NOFX Relevance Score**: 3

## Abstract

We investigate the first-passage dynamics and energetic cost of a Brownian particle subject to finite-time stochastic resetting in a bounded one-dimensional domain with two absorbing boundaries. Unlike conventional instantaneous resetting, the particle is returned to a prescribed resetting position through a finite-duration process driven by a linear trapping potential. By solving the corresponding backward equations, we derive analytical expressions for the mean global first-passage time and the splitting probabilities. Our results show that the finite duration of the return process can substantially modify the dependence of the mean global first-passage time on the resetting rate. In particular, we identify a critical trapping strength above which resetting can accelerate the search process, and characterize how the range of favorable resetting positions depends on the trapping strength. We further characterize the energetic cost of resetting by deriving the generating function of the total work and obtaining an analytical expression for its mean value. For the linear trapping potential considered here, we establish an exact relation between the mean total work and the mean cumulative return time. This relation allows us to establish a quantitative trade-off between the mean global first-passage time and the energetic expenditure, revealing how the finite duration of the return process affects search efficiency. Brownian dynamics simulations confirm our theoretical predictions.
