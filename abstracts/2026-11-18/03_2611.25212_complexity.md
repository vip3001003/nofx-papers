# A Symmetry-Stratified Framework for Phase Transitions: Thermodynamic States, Critical Dynamics, and Spectral Mechanical Signatures

- **arXiv**: [2611.25212](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25212)
- **Date**: 2026-11-18
- **Category**: complexity
- **Authors**: Pengfei Zhu, Leiming Du
- **NOFX Relevance Score**: 3

## Abstract

Structural symmetry, thermodynamic phase stability, and elastic-wave response provide related but distinct descriptions of a transforming solid. We formulate a symmetry-stratified framework that connects these descriptions through a finite-dimensional internal state and a parent-invariant free energy. Stabilizers classify structural states, whereas free-energy comparisons and stability conditions determine equilibrium branches and dissipative evolution. Equivariance ensures that the frozen elastic tangent inherits the structural stabilizer. Equality requires additional identifiability conditions. Little-group representations and degenerate perturbation theory then determine whether an acoustic doublet splits and the first nonzero splitting order $p$. On a selected continuous branch with order-parameter exponent $β$, a regular, observable acoustic gap scales as $|t|^{pβ}$, provided critical self-energies and structural relaxation do not dominate the measurement. Explicit tensorial and cubic-to-tetragonal realizations yield linear and quadratic splitting channels, together with directionally protected degeneracies. Separate numerical benchmarks examine free-energy reconstruction, coexistence, elastic compatibility, finite-temperature spectra, and controlled critical readouts. Reanalysis of NiTi measurements illustrates effective elastic degeneracy, thermal-history dependence, and the predictive value of independently measured phase fractions. Most importantly, the framework identifies the constitutive and measurement assumptions required to infer structural changes from mechanical spectra.
