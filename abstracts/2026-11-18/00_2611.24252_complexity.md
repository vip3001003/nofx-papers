# Neither graphs nor hypergraphs: structural moments of higher-order interactions determine stochastic social-impact dynamics

- **arXiv**: [2611.24252](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24252)
- **Date**: 2026-11-18
- **Category**: complexity
- **Authors**: Emanuele Cozzo
- **NOFX Relevance Score**: 0

## Abstract

Whether higher-order interactions can be reduced to simpler representations is a question about the dynamics they drive, not only about their structure: what can be discarded is whatever the dynamics does not see. We make this precise for binary-state stochastic dynamics in which each agent, when updated, interacts with a randomly selected group and changes state according to the fraction of disagreeing members, as in generalized voter and social-impact models on hypergraphs. We ask which statistics of the group-interaction process determine the resulting Markov dynamics. For response functions that are polynomials of degree $q$, the answer is a hierarchy of size-weighted inclusion moments, which we call structural moments, involving at most $q$ neighbors at a time, regardless of how large the groups are. These moments are not only sufficient but also necessary: two interaction structures produce identical dynamics for all such rules if and only if their moments coincide. More generally, each class of rules selects its own minimal structural description, ranging from weighted pairwise links for linear rules to the full distribution of co-membership by group size for arbitrary rules. For voter-type rules, the same moments also reconstruct exact pairwise copying representations of these dynamics.
