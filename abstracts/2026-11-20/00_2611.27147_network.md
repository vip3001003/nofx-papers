# Adaptive Collective Decision-Making in Signed Networks

- **arXiv**: [2611.27147](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27147)
- **Date**: 2026-11-20
- **Category**: network
- **Authors**: Vishnudatta Thota, Aditi Saxena, Twinkle Tripathy et al.
- **NOFX Relevance Score**: 0

## Abstract

Collective decision-making often unfolds in networks where cooperation and antagonism coexist. Such signed interactions arise in social, biological, and robotic systems, and can lead to qualitatively different collective outcomes: consensus, polarization, or clustering. Existing signed network models typically explain these outcomes separately, leaving open how they can emerge within a single dynamical framework. Here, we introduce a generalized Laplacian flow for bi-partitioned signed networks, a class of networks that allows cooperative and antagonistic interactions both within and across two prescribed groups. The proposed model includes an adaptive social effort state, interpreted as a leaky memory of persistent signed network disagreement, which enables bounded and nontrivial steady-states beyond classical signed Laplacian flows. We derive conditions that determine whether the network reaches consensus, polarization, or clustering based on the spectral structure of the generalized Laplacian and effective resistance matrices. The framework applies beyond structurally balanced, weakly balanced, and quasi-structurally balanced networks, and reveals how changes in signed interaction strengths can shift a network between collective decision regimes. We validate the theory on stochastic block model networks, a judicial voting network, and a U.S. senate bill co-sponsorship network. Altogether, our results provide a structural and dynamical account of how mixed cooperative and antagonistic relationships shape emergent collective behaviors.
