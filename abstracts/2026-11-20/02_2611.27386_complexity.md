# Pushy Random Walk on the Bethe lattice: a Galton-Watson perspective

- **arXiv**: [2611.27386](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27386)
- **Date**: 2026-11-20
- **Category**: complexity
- **Authors**: Jérémie Klinger
- **NOFX Relevance Score**: 2

## Abstract

Pushy random walks are minimal models of transport in deformable media, in which the walker displaces the obstacles it bumps into rather than being blocked by them. On the Bethe lattice, we show that the walkable structure dynamically created by a pushy walker can be generated a priori as an explicit multi-type Galton-Watson tree, so that the walker reduces to a simple random walk on a static random tree, and its escape to infinity coincides with the survival of the underlying branching process. Standard generating-function tools then give direct access to the escape properties of these walks. For the Sokoban walk, which pushes a single obstacle, we recover the known critical density $ρ_c = 1-(z-1)^{-2}$ and the behavior of the escape probability close to $ρ_c$, and derive its small-density asymptotics. For the second-order pushy walk, which can push trains of two obstacles, we establish the conjectured $ρ_c = 1-(z-1)^{-3}$, and compute the escape probability at all densities. Beyond escape, the same framework yields the statistics of the trace of trapped walkers: the distribution of the maximal depth follows from the extinction probabilities, while the distributions of the visited volume and of the number of dead ends are encoded in explicit functional equations for their generating functions, which we invert numerically. All predictions agree with Monte Carlo simulations.
