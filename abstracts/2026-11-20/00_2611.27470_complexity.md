# Adaptive obstacle deposition drives dynamical localization in chase-and-escape dynamics

- **arXiv**: [2611.27470](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27470)
- **Date**: 2026-11-20
- **Category**: complexity
- **Authors**: R. G. Rossatto, Amin Bakhshandeh, H. Arial Alvarez et al.
- **NOFX Relevance Score**: 0

## Abstract

Most pursuit--evasion models assume a fixed environment, although defensive actions can leave persistent structures that affect subsequent encounters. We study a two-dimensional lattice model in which threatened escapers deposit permanent obstacles, coupling capture kinetics to the geometry explored by their pursuers. Increasing the deposition probability enhances escaper survival and strongly suppresses chaser transport. For dilute chasers, the mean-squared-displacement exponent decreases from values near unity towards zero, trajectories contract to a few lattice spacings and the localized fraction rises sharply. The localization response becomes weaker as chaser abundance increases. Time-resolved obstacle densities support a kinetic explanation: rapid capture depletes obstacle-producing escapers before a comparably confining environment can develop. At intermediate parameters, broad or bimodal distributions of trajectory radii and MSD exponents reveal simultaneous mobile and localized subpopulations. Thus deposition redistributes trajectories between distinct dynamical behaviors, producing heterogeneity beyond a uniform slowdown. We summarize the coupled survival and transport response in an empirical map of mobile, heterogeneous and trapping-dominated regimes, interpreted as finite-time crossovers. These results identify the lifetime of the obstacle-producing population as a key control of adaptive localization: a stronger instantaneous pursuit pressure can weaken confinement by shortening the period over which the environment is modified.
