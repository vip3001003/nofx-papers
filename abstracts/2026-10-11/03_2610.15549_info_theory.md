# Rate-Distortion Theory of Gaussian Mixtures via Posterior Label Sampling

- **arXiv**: [2610.15549](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.15549)
- **Date**: 2026-10-11
- **Category**: info_theory
- **Authors**: Weichen Wang, Deniz Gündüz
- **NOFX Relevance Score**: 3

## Abstract

We study the mean-squared error (MSE) rate-distortion function (RDF) of finite Gaussian mixture models (GMMs) in $\mathbb{R}^d$, for which neither the RDF nor an optimal test channel is known in closed form. It can be sandwiched between the genie-aided conditional RDF $R_{\mathbf{X} | T}(D)$, which can be characterized in closed-form for a GMM, and $R_{\mathbf{X} | T}(D)+I(\mathbf{X};T)$, where $T$ denotes the latent component label. We show that the upper bound can be achieved operationally by the proposed Gibbs-relabelling method (GRM), which conveys a label from the posterior $P_{T|\mathbf{X}}$ through \textit{channel simulation} at rate $I(\mathbf{X};T)$, followed by vector quantization of the resulting exactly Gaussian conditional sources by reverse water-filling. Using the Shannon lower bound (SLB), we prove that GRM attains the SLB, and hence the RDF, of a general finite mixture if and only if every component attains its own SLB. For a GMM this holds throughout the fully active interval $D/d<\min_{k,m}λ_{k,m}$, where $λ_{k,m}$ are the eigenvalues of the component covariances, and for scalar GMMs the condition is also necessary. Since GRM cannot operate below the label cost $I(\mathbf{X};T)$, we propose a complementary stochastic scalar quantization (SSQ) scheme for low rates, which simulates an $M$-ary reconstruction index. We show that at low rates SSQ with $M\geq 2$ matches the Shannon RDF to first order: $R(D_{\max}-δ)=δ/(2τ^2)+o(δ)$, where $τ^2$ is a directional sub-Gaussian proxy of the mixture, and quantify the price of a finite codebook through a strict rate gap at high rates and a distortion floor whose joint scaling in $M$ and number of mixture components is characterized. Numerical results illustrate both regimes and the finite-blocklength cost of channel simulation.
