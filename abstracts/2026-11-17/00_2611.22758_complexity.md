# Effects of probe size, shape, and probe-surface interactions in imaging multilayer island growth, coalescence, and formation of a smooth film

- **arXiv**: [2611.22758](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22758)
- **Date**: 2026-11-17
- **Category**: complexity
- **Authors**: Ismael S. S. Carrasco, Tiago J. Oliveira, Fábio D. A. Aarão Reis
- **NOFX Relevance Score**: 0

## Abstract

Recent studies of deposition onto weakly interacting substrates have revealed multilayer island growth, followed by smooth-film formation upon island coalescence. However, for C60 films, the roughness peak measured by atomic force microscopy (AFM) was significantly lower than that obtained by X-ray reflectometry (XRR), suggesting tip-sample convolution effects. We investigate this problem using kinetic Monte Carlo (KMC) simulations of such films and models of probe-surface interactions for AFM imaging modes. To model the non-contact mode, surface profiles are transformed by averaging heights over regions matching the probe-tip width. A roughness peak remains if the probe width L is smaller than the average island distance, but its height decreases with L and its position is slightly displaced. In the contact-mode model with cylindrical probes and hard-core interactions, the peak also decreases and shifts significantly to smaller thicknesses, indicating apparently faster island coalescence. Thus, SPM images under these conditions may underestimate film-thickness nonuniformity in deposits with coalescing islands. Instead, models of sharp pyramidal or conical probes with hard-core interactions produce deviations in the roughness peaks only for large L, although such sharp tips are difficult to realize experimentally. A geometric model of cuboid island growth and coalescence quantitatively confirms the effects of contact modes and probe shapes observed in the simulations. The results suggest that post-growth processes, such as island coarsening and dewetting, may have occurred in the recently studied C60 film between the XRR and AFM measurements.
