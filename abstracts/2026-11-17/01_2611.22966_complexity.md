# Polymer adsorption on vicinal surfaces

- **arXiv**: [2611.22966](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.22966)
- **Date**: 2026-11-17
- **Category**: complexity
- **Authors**: Nathann T. Rodrigues, Tiago J. Oliveira
- **NOFX Relevance Score**: 1

## Abstract

While polymer adsorption is theoretically well established on flat surfaces --- and somewhat understood on rough surfaces --- the common and practically relevant case of nearly flat stepped surfaces remains poorly studied. Here, we address this gap via extensive simulations of flexible polymer chains grafted at the step edge of vicinal surfaces. For wide terraces, the polymer-step interaction yields a stable step-attached (SA) phase, alongside the desorbed and terrace-adsorbed (TA) phases, all of them separated by continuous transition lines that meet at a multicritical point previously unanticipated in polymer thermodynamics. This scenario is observed for terrace widths $\mathcal{W} \gtrsim 3 R_g^{(2D)}$, being $R_g^{(2D)}$ the radius of gyration of TA polymers. Simpler phase diagrams, absent this multicriticality, are found for $\mathcal{W} \lesssim 3 R_g^{(2D)}$, where a simple crossover exists between the SA and TA phases. The presence of kinks at the steps does not alter these behaviors. Besides their theoretical relevance, these findings unveil an interesting route to produce polymer nanowires and control polymer conformation via step attachment-detachment on designed stepped surfaces.
