# Universal Work Extraction from Individual Sequences by Finite--State Information Engines

- **arXiv**: [2611.33984](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33984)
- **Date**: 2026-11-25
- **Category**: complexity
- **Authors**: Neri Merhav
- **NOFX Relevance Score**: 1

## Abstract

We consider information engines that interact with a heat bath at temperature $T$, a work reservoir, and a tape carrying an individual (deterministic) sequence $x$ over a finite alphabet $\X$. An engine is a cascade of autonomous ratchets with fixed energy levels and detailed--balance dynamics, each processing segments of $m$ tape cells and having finitely many internal states. We prove a converse bound on the extractable work in terms of the empirical block entropies of $x$,and show that it is achievable \emph{universally}, by engines whose energy levels do not depend on $x$. The maximal work per symbol is $kT[\ln|\X|-\bar H(x)]$, where $k$ is Boltzmann's constant and $\bar H(x)$ is the asymptotic normalized aligned block entropy of $x$, which equals its block Lempel--Ziv complexity, and the minimal work needed to erase $x$ is $kT\bar H(x)$ per symbol. Underlying these results is a two--way correspondence with universal coding: energy levels define a code, and a suitably operated cascade extracts $kT$ times $\ln|\X|$ per symbol minus the code length; conversely, a fluctuation identity shows that every engine fixed in advance defines a code. Hence, converses of universal coding apply verbatim to engines; e.g., the optimal redundancy of memoryless universal engines is given by the Shtarkov sum.
