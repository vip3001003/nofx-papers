# Rotation curves of dwarf galaxies and dark matter as a condensate of $q$-deformed bosons: reconstruction and fits of the deformation profile

- **arXiv**: [2611.33999](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33999)
- **Date**: 2026-11-25
- **Category**: complexity
- **Authors**: Mahnaz Maleki, Asef Kheirandish, Morteza Nattagh Najafi et al.
- **NOFX Relevance Score**: 0

## Abstract

Dark-matter-dominated dwarf galaxies are a sensitive testing ground for models in which dark matter is a self-interacting Bose-Einstein condensate (BEC). In the Thomas-Fermi limit the standard BEC halo has the density profile $ρ\propto\sin(kr)/(kr)$, so that the shape of its rotation curve is fixed up to two scale parameters. Here we consider a condensate of $q$-deformed bosons. We show that, in the Thomas-Fermi limit, the deformation enters the structure of the halo only through the chemical potential of the condensate; consequently, a spatially uniform deformation parameter leaves the standard BEC profile unchanged. In every galaxy $χ$ is depressed in the inner region, $kr\lesssim1$, relative to the outer halo, where it is approximately constant in most cases: the standard condensate describes the outer halo, while the core requires a weaker deformation, i.e., $q$ closer to unity. The profiles share this qualitative shape, but neither the depth nor the width of the depression is universal. Modelling the depression by a Gaussian, with two additional parameters, we fit the rotation curves with rms residuals of $0.6$--$2.6$~km\,s$^{-1}$, against $1.6$--$8.5$~km\,s$^{-1}$ for the standard condensate. According to the Bayesian information criterion, the deformed condensate is preferred over the standard condensate, the Navarro-Frenk-White profile, and the Burkert profile in four galaxies, is indistinguishable from the best of them in two, and is disfavoured in two. We discuss the limitations of these results, in particular the absence of error bars in the data, the neglect of baryons, and the phenomenological status of a position-dependent deformation.
