# Strong Collective Dynamics Hidden behind a Nearly Free Single-Particle Spectrum

- **arXiv**: [2611.10636](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10636)
- **Date**: 2026-11-07
- **Category**: complexity
- **Authors**: Zhixuan Zhao, Sicheng He, Hanxiang Di et al.
- **NOFX Relevance Score**: 0

## Abstract

Single-particle spectroscopy has long served as a criterion for the strength of electronic correlations: strong collective motion is expected to shift and broaden spectral peaks and to renormalize the dispersion. Here we show that this correspondence can fail on the same microscopic orbital. The mechanism relies on local interference between two resonant side orbitals: one suppresses low-energy real transitions into the interacting environment, while the other suppresses spectral-weight backflow to the source orbital; virtual mixing nevertheless transfers the environmental spin polarization to the source. We prove a rigorous relative spectral bound that is uniform in environment size and interaction strength and holds for arbitrary stationary states; the protection becomes stronger as the relative frequency resolution improves. In a fully quantum two-cell device, a collective mode softens by 60%, whereas the particle intensity at the free peak changes by only 3.6 parts per million. Quantum Monte Carlo shows that the transferred spin response grows with spatial correlations and is quantitatively predicted by the free-band polarization. These results recast the meaning of a simple particle spectrum and provide a design principle for quantum probes that combine predictable particle spectra with strong collective sensitivity.
