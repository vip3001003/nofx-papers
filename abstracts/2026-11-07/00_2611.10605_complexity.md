# Hyperuniformity in active matter

- **Date**: 2026-11-07
- **Category**: complexity
- **Authors**: Hartmut Löwen, Erdal C. Oğuz

## Abstract

Disordered hyperuniform systems are characterized by an anomalously strong suppression of long-wavelength density fluctuations. As a result, these unusual materials behave as though virtually incompressible, even though the particle arrangement itself can remain fully disordered at short range. This peculiar state of matter has been the topic of intense research in physics and mathematics in recent years. Another flourishing research arena is active matter, composed of self-propelled agents that convert energy into motion. While the two fields remained largely disjunct for a long time, the paper by Lei and coworkers (2019) created a link between hyperuniformity and active matter by demonstrating that interacting circle swimmers self-organize into disordered hyperuniform structures. Inspired by this work, many other examples of hyperuniformity in active matter have been identified. We shall describe the impact and outgrowth of this development, and give a brief perspective on further links between hyperuniformity and active matter.
