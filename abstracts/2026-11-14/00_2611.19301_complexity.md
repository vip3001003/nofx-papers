# Finding Low-Energy States of the Bernasconi Model via Simulated Transverse-Field Quantum Dynamics

- **arXiv**: [2611.19301](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19301)
- **Date**: 2026-11-14
- **Category**: complexity
- **Authors**: Shaashwat Sagar Kale, Rudranil Basu
- **NOFX Relevance Score**: 0

## Abstract

The Low-Autocorrelation Binary Sequence (LABS) problem, the ground-state problem of the Bernasconi model, has a rugged landscape with deep, isolated local minima. We study real-time transverse-field dynamics initialized in these minima as a means of reaching lower-energy configurations. Using GPU statevector simulation for N = 20-30 spins, we compare the escape probability per verified quantum sample with the success rate per energy evaluation of steepest-descent kicks, random restarts and tabu search. Using the same field and sampling window across all system sizes, we find that, relative to the best classical baseline, the sampler achieves a factor of 11.9-35.2 for the deepest traps and 13.4-27.2 for the next energy tier. These ratios describe a constant-factor improvement in sample efficiency over the sizes studied. At the adopted field, broad Hamming-space spreading coexists with a strong preference for low-energy states. This regime follows a sharp increase in displacement as the field is raised. To connect this behaviour to the landscape, a short-time expansion relates the onset of escape to target distance and intermediate energies. Bounds on isotropic spin flips and on the free transverse mixer quantify the role of the Bernasconi potential in the observed transfer. At N = 28, the ground-state probability at t = 30 exceeds a canonical cost prediction fitted to the same mean energy by a factor of about 1.9. The cost of generating each quantum sample is assessed separately through a hardware estimate, which places a direct circuit implementation two to three orders of magnitude beyond present processor capabilities.
