# A Neural Density Functional for Spatial Structure and Orientational Ordering in Two-Dimensional Lattice Hard Rods

- **arXiv**: [2611.19553](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19553)
- **Date**: 2026-11-14
- **Category**: complexity
- **Authors**: Paul Bitzer, Jens Weimar, Martin Oettel
- **NOFX Relevance Score**: 0

## Abstract

We study spatial structure and orientational ordering of horizontal and vertical hard rods on a two-dimensional square lattice using neural classical density functional theory. We construct two convolutional architectures that exploit the symmetry relating the rod orientations: a direct-$c^{(1)}$ network (learning the map between the first functional derivative of the excess free energy and the inhomogeneous density, $c^{(1)} \leftrightarrow ρ$) and a $Φ$-network (learning directly the map between free energy and density, $F_\text{ex} \leftrightarrow ρ$). Both networks use exactly the same inhomogeneous grand-canonical Monte Carlo density profiles and one-body direct correlation targets. Both neural representations substantially improve the predicted density profiles relative to the Lafuente--Cuesta lattice functional. The resulting free energy landscapes, however, differ between the two representations. Furthermore for the direct-$c^{(1)}$ network the integration along different density paths produces different free energies. As a result, the transition to nematic ordering for rods with aspect ratio 8 is strongly first order for the direct-$c^{(1)}$ network while only weakly discontinuous for the $Φ$-network. The latter is a reasonable approximation to the continuous transition found in simulations, demonstrating that subtle entropic free energy differences are accessible in neural cDFT.
