# Finite-time Mpemba crossings with imperfect preparation

- **arXiv**: [2610.41591](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41591)
- **Date**: 2026-10-30
- **Category**: complexity
- **Authors**: Mesfin Taye
- **NOFX Relevance Score**: 0

## Abstract

Two preparations evolving under the same thermal Markov generator can reverse their ordering in relative entropy even when the hotter state retains a nonzero slow-mode component. We derive a sufficient finite-time crossing condition for arbitrary finite irreducible reversible systems. The condition compares the preparations' weights in the entire slowest eigenspace, including degeneracies, and gives an explicit upper bound using the separation from the next distinct decay rate. Exact finite-amplitude entropy comparisons also yield all-mode enclosures and bounds based on a retained spectrum with a controlled omitted tail. A preparation-error inequality converts finite-time sign margins into explicit probability tolerances; extensions account for temperature, generator, and stationary-reference uncertainty. We test the bounds on 2000 predefined network draws with four graph families and 4--64 states. There are 86 numerically located crossings, of which 45 satisfy the simple sufficient condition. In a selected eight-state example, retaining two nonstationary modes certifies a crossing before 1.55163, improving a certified slow-sector endpoint of 2.53951; the located crossing is near 1.26943. A reflecting diffusion in an asymmetric potential supplies a separate physical example, with systematic mesh refinement. Independent interval arithmetic certifies selected spectral bounds, finite-grid first crossings, and an entire preparation-temperature rectangle in a three-state benchmark. The theorem does not require exact slow-mode cancellation, while its numerical sharpness and detection coverage remain model dependent. These results concern relative-entropy relaxation and do not predict water-freezing times.
