# A feedback-driven central limit theorem with $q$-Gaussian limits

- **arXiv**: [2611.20201](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20201)
- **Date**: 2026-11-15
- **Category**: complexity
- **Authors**: G. Baris Bagci, Thomas Oikonomou
- **NOFX Relevance Score**: 1

## Abstract

We establish a central limit theorem, the feedback-driven central limit theorem, for sums whose summands are independent noise variables multiplied by an amplitude that depends on the preceding normalized sum $y$. The summands are martingale differences whose conditional variance, $c+b\,y^{2}$, depends quadratically on $y$, with feedback strength $b$; unlike in the martingale central limit theorem, this conditional variance does not settle to a constant. The noise has finite variance; for positive feedback we assume, as in Lyapunov's theorem, a finite third moment, and for negative feedback bounded noise, so that the conditional variance stays nonnegative. For positive feedback, the normalized sum converges to a Student law, equivalently a $q$-Gaussian with $1<q<2$, independently of the noise distribution. For negative feedback, it converges to a compactly supported density, which is a $q$-Gaussian with $q<1$ when $-\tfrac12<b<0$. The Gaussian law, $q=1$, is recovered at zero feedback, while the Cauchy law, $q=2$, is obtained only as a limiting case, in a triangular array in which the feedback grows slowly with the number of terms. Thus every $q<2$ is obtained for a fixed feedback strength, and $q=2$ as a limit. We further show that the balance identity behind the theorem singles out quadratic feedback: a $q$-Gaussian with $q<2$ satisfies it only for a quadratic conditional variance, and no $q$-Gaussian with $2\le q<3$ satisfies it for any conditional variance. The proofs use the method of moments for negative feedback and a Wasserstein coupling argument for positive feedback, including cases in which the limiting variance is infinite.
