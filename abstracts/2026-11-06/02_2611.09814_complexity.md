# Arrival-time problem in the quantum information era - observation of a Zeno crossover in monitored arrival-time distributions

- **arXiv**: [2611.09814](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09814)
- **Date**: 2026-11-06
- **Category**: complexity
- **Authors**: Yousef Mafi, K. G. S. H. Gunawardana, Kim Pöyhönen et al.
- **NOFX Relevance Score**: 2

## Abstract

When does a quantum particle arrive at a detector? The temporal distributions of itinerant quantum particles have been debated since the birth of quantum mechanics. Here we study the arrival-time problem in periodically monitored spin chains and quantum circuits. The arrival dynamics exhibits two regimes, the quantum Zeno and the low-monitoring regime, separated by an optimal monitoring period set by the single-particle bandwidth. In support of the theory, on superconducting quantum hardware, we measure arrival-time distributions for single and multiple interacting particles in one- and two-dimensional circuits. The measured distributions resolve both regimes and agree closely with quantum-trajectory theory. The crossover persists in interacting many-particle systems: the optimal monitoring point is preserved while many-body interference reshapes the distributions. Zeno suppression likewise persists in two-dimensional geometry. Together, these results show that the two regimes are a generic feature of monitored propagation rather than a single-particle effect. To our knowledge, our work constitutes the first experimental measurement of arrival-time distributions in a monitored quantum transport setting, addressing one of the longest-standing problems in quantum mechanics.
