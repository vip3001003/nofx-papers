# Recurrent losers: A network approach to cover-bidding patterns in public procurement

- **arXiv**: [2611.20527](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20527)
- **Date**: 2026-11-16
- **Category**: network
- **Authors**: Isabela Villamil, János Kertész
- **NOFX Relevance Score**: 0

## Abstract

We develop and apply a network-based method to examine patterns consistent with cover bidding within procurement markets, using only bidder participation and winner identities. A statistical test identifies relationships in which firms win unusually often when particular competitors also bid. These relationships form a directed graph with links from recurrent losers to recurrent winners. We use maximum $k$-core to classify possible organizational forms of cover bidding and propose tender exposure -- the share of contested tenders involving a reported relationship -- as a market-level indicator of potential cover-bidding risk. Applied to 200,065 contracts procured by district engineering offices of the Philippine Department of Public Works and Highways (DPWH) from 2017 to 2025, covering three congressional terms, the method identifies 8,728 directed relationships across 407 of 564 markets. The $k$-core classes differ in network breadth, relationship depth, and bidder roles. In simpler networks, each recurrent loser is usually tied to a single winner, while highly interconnected networks involve multiple recurrent winners and broader groups of bidders that rarely win. Some of the deepest relationships and highest levels of tender exposure occur in intermediate networks, showing how relationships can reach much of a market without extensive interconnectedness. Offices named in official investigations more often contain highly interconnected networks than other offices. Two of the three offices referred for bid rigging are among the twelve in our ranking with the highest exposure in 2023-2025.
