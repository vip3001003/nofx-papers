# Long-term Coexistence of Epidemics and Risk Awareness: Impacts of Adaptive Human Response and Fatigue

- **arXiv**: [2607.18301](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.18301)
- **Date**: 2026-07-16
- **Category**: bio_inspired
- **Authors**: Mozzamil Mohammed, Abdallah Alsammani
- **NOFX Relevance Score**: 0

## Abstract

Adaptive human behavior is widely recognized as a key determinant of epidemic dynamics, yet remains elusive to incorporate into epidemic models in a mechanistically transparent and analytically tractable manner. Here, we introduce a modeling framework in which population-level awareness is represented explicitly as a dynamic behavioral state that governs individual accessibility for interaction, rather than modifying intrinsic transmission probabilities. Awareness emerges endogenously in response to infection prevalence and decays through behavioral fatigue, generating a closed feedback loop between disease dynamics and social accessibility. This framework reveals that awareness-driven accessibility alone can produce transient epidemic waves, while epidemic trajectories ultimately converge to a long-term coexistence of infection and risk awareness. By separating behavioral accessibility from biological transmission processes, we show that awareness responsiveness and behavioral fatigue jointly regulate both transient epidemic structure and long-term endemic burden without altering the epidemic invasion threshold. These results identify awareness-driven accessibility as a fundamental and previously underrepresented component of epidemic dynamics, providing a mechanistic basis for explicitly integrating adaptive human behavior into epidemic modeling and informing public health preparedness for emerging and re-emerging infectious diseases.
