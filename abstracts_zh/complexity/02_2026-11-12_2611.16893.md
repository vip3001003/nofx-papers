# A deterministic theory of kinetic fluctuations in long-range interacting homogeneous systems without collective effects

- **arXiv**: [2611.16893](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16893)
- **日期**: 2026-11-12
- **分类**: complexity
- **作者**: Anwar El Rhirhayi, Jean-Baptiste Fouvry, Julien Barré
- **NOFX相关度**: 2

## 摘要

Finite-$N$ fluctuations govern the slow relaxation of long-range interacting systems, yet accessing their full statistics demands huge ensembles of costly simulations. We show that, in homogeneous systems without collective effects, this statistical problem collapses onto just three deterministic kinetic equations. Starting from the Landau-Dean-Kawasaki equation, i.e. the stochastic counterpart of the large deviation principle for the Landau equation, we derive the infinite hierarchy of $n$-point moments of the empirical distribution function at leading order in $1/N$. We prove that it is solved exactly by a quasi-Gaussian closure. The resulting minimal Landau fluctuation hierarchy is formed by the Landau equation, a Lyapunov equation for the two-point moment, and a kinetic equation for the three-point moment. From these three equations, all higher-order moments follow algebraically. The hierarchy exactly preserves the collisional invariants and their fluctuations, and predicts the full probability distribution of observables in the central fluctuation regime. Benchmarked against $N$-body realizations, it reproduces the mean, variance and higher-order moments with an accuracy that improves systematically with $N$. A single deterministic time-integration of three coupled partial differential equations thus replaces large ensembles of simulations, for any large $N$.
