# Amplifying or Redistributing Coupling: Synchronization in Sparse Adaptive Oscillator Networks

- **arXiv**: [2611.24268](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24268)
- **日期**: 2026-11-18
- **分类**: complexity
- **作者**: Yousef Jamali
- **NOFX相关度**: 1

## 摘要

Synchronization -- different units falling into a common rhythm -- matters in systems ranging from electrical generators to populations of neurons. But what happens when the connections that coordinate their timing can also change? If two units become more closely aligned and their connection strengthens, that extra influence can either increase the total coupling received by a unit or take a larger share of a fixed total. We examine these alternatives in a mathematical network of oscillators, each representing a repeating cycle, connected by sparse directed links. We compare constant connections with two adaptive rules that favor phase-aligned neighbors: unconstrained Hebbian coupling and homeostatic normalization, which keeps the total incoming effective coupling of each oscillator constant. Exact relationships establish this conservation and show that the mean Hebbian weight relaxes toward a value set by alignment along the network links. In matched numerical experiments, unconstrained adaptation reaches a given level of collective synchrony at weaker imposed coupling than fixed connections, whereas the normalized network requires stronger coupling within the same observation window. Longer observations reveal distinct individual trajectories, including increasing, sustained, and declining coherence. The central lesson is that the collective consequences of adaptation depend not only on which links are strengthened, but also on whether their strengthening adds influence or redistributes it.
