# Color Through Time: Distributions, Sparsity and Dominance in Western Painting

- **arXiv**: [2611.23139](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.23139)
- **日期**: 2026-11-17
- **分类**: complexity
- **作者**: Ortensia Forni, Alexandre Darmon, Michael Benzaquen
- **NOFX相关度**: 0

## 摘要

We investigate the evolution of color in Western painting using a large corpus of digitized artworks. Each painting is represented by its distribution over a fixed palette of colors in a perceptually uniform space. We compare the color distributions of historical periods and artistic movements through their Jensen-Shannon distances, and introduce two interpretable descriptors, sparsity and dominance, that quantify how many colors are used and how unevenly they are represented. We find a historical transition from dark, earthy palettes toward a broader and more saturated chromatic range, unfolding through distinct shifts from the nineteenth century onward. Periods evolve smoothly over time, whereas artistic movements display increasingly distinct chromatic signatures beyond chronology. In the sparsity-dominance plane, painting follows a two-stage historical trajectory, first broadening the range of colors used and later increasing color dominance. Comparison with natural images reveals distinct ways in which artistic palettes depart from nature, either through the colors they use or through how broadly and evenly those colors are distributed. Finally, two surveys on generated images show that judgments of harmonious color balance concentrate around two complementary regimes: many colors arranged in a strong hierarchy, or few colors used evenly.
