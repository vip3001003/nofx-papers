# Mechanical acclimation of bacterial growth and division across generations

- **arXiv**: [2611.18273](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.18273)
- **日期**: 2026-11-13
- **分类**: complexity
- **作者**: Chenyu Jin, G. H. Philipp Nguyen, René Wittmann et al.
- **NOFX相关度**: 1

## 摘要

Bacteria frequently encounter mechanical confinement in natural and engineered environments, yet how an abrupt change in mechanical conditions reorganizes growth and division across generations remains poorly understood. Using single-cell time-lapse microscopy, we tracked Escherichia coli across generations following transfer from liquid culture to glass-attached or glass-gel-confined conditions. This mechanical confinement causes an immediate reduction in elongation rate, followed by a multi-generational mechanical acclimation process whose dynamics depend systematically on confinement strength. During this relaxation, division behavior becomes more heterogeneous and cells temporarily depart from canonical adder-like size regulation, exhibiting timer-like correlation signatures. Using a stochastic cell-cycle model that resolves the generation-dependent correlation signatures of different size-control strategies, we show that changes in elongation rate alone cannot account for the observed transition in growth-division coordination. Despite this transient reorganization, the birth-size distribution remains bounded and largely preserves its shape throughout acclimation. Notably, adder-like regulation is restored before the elongation rate fully recovers, revealing distinct relaxation timescales for growth and size-control dynamics. These results establish bacterial size control under mechanical confinement as a dynamical nonequilibrium process in which growth and division can transiently reorganize while global size homeostasis is maintained.
