# Basic introduction to Freidlin-Wentzell theory

- **arXiv**: [2611.19916](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.19916)
- **日期**: 2026-11-15
- **分类**: complexity
- **作者**: Jiawei Yan
- **NOFX相关度**: 2

## 摘要

Freidlin-Wentzell theory provides the quantitative foundation for rare, noise-induced transitions in stochastic dynamical systems, predicting optimal transition pathways, the exponential scaling of mean escape times, and the long-term occupancy of competing attractors in metastable systems. Here we present a pedagogical, physics-oriented review built around a unified Hamiltonian framework: starting from the infinitesimal generator of a continuous-time Markov process, we derive the local Hamiltonian, obtain the Lagrangian and action functional via Legendre-Fenchel duality, and construct the quasi-potential landscape. We apply this streamlined formalism across weak-noise diffusions, Markov jump processes, weakly interacting particle ensembles, and nonequilibrium stochastic fields, alongside exact conditions for macroscopic coarse-graining. Along the way, we highlight how broken detailed balance drives forward and reverse transition paths apart, explain where local linear stability and Gaussian approximations break down, and assemble multi-attractor landscapes using combinatorial $W$-graphs. Finally, we bridge the analytical theory to modern numerical solvers, including ordered upwind methods, geometric minimum action algorithms, and neural-network parameterizations, and provide an accompanying GPU-accelerated Python package.
