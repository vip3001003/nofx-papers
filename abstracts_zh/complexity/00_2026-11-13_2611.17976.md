# A Multiobjective Optimization Framework for Sensor Array Design in Magnetocardiography

- **arXiv**: [2611.17976](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17976)
- **日期**: 2026-11-13
- **分类**: complexity
- **作者**: Kesavaraja C, Sammita Kashyap, Korak Biswas et al.
- **NOFX相关度**: 0

## 摘要

Magnetocardiography (MCG) noninvasively measures cardiac magnetic fields. Conventional cryogenic systems use fixed multichannel arrays, whereas room-temperature sensors enable flexible placement to reduce array size and measurement redundancy. The present study aims at developing a scheme to identify the optimal sensor placements by utilizing three methodological sensor optimization frameworks such as physics driven, experimental data driven and a hybrid combination of both. The physics-driven method optimizes continuous planar coordinates using D-optimality, spatial correlation, and leastsquares error. The data-driven method uses measured spatiotemporal fields and pole-geometry metrics, while the hybrid method integrates both approaches. The evaluation is performed using training objective, Pearson correlation, normalized root-meansquare error (NRMSE) and pole separation(mm), orientation(°) and trajectory(mm) errors, where physics-driven optimization identified 16 sensors with a correlation of 0.9914 and an NRMSE of 0.0139, with pole separation, orientation, and trajectory errors of 3.71 mm, 1.38°, and 3.55 mm, respectively. Data-driven optimization shows diminishing improvements beyond 17 sensors, achieving correlation 0.9840, NRMSE 0.0035, and pole errors of approximately 4.30 mm, 1.70°, and 6.80 mm. The hybrid method achieves correlation 0.9945 and NRMSE 0.0201 with 14 sensors, reducing sensor count by 12.5% relative to the physics-driven configuration. Its pole errors of 6.50 mm, 2.31°, and 7.50 mm satisfy the study-defined thresholds, balancing array compactness with magnetic-field morphology preservation. These results show that optimized placement helps alleviate the complexity of data redundancy and enhances the performance of MCG as a routine cost-effective clinical measurement.
