# Energetic costs of reaching the physical limits of sensing

- **arXiv**: [2611.14145](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.14145)
- **日期**: 2026-11-10
- **分类**: complexity
- **作者**: Albert Alonso, Julius B. Kirkegaard, Robert G. Endres
- **NOFX相关度**: 2

## 摘要

Cells sense chemical gradients by absorbing ligand molecules, a nonequilibrium process that necessarily produces entropy. But how many molecules must a cell absorb, and therefore energy dissipate, to approach the physical limits of sensing? We derive closed-form expressions relating gradient sensing uncertainty to the number of absorbed molecules, which we link to entropy production. Unlike concentration sensing, which depends only on the total count, gradient sensing must resolve the angular asymmetry of the absorbed flux, adding a bounded dipole-contrast factor set by how strongly the cell absorbs. In the strong-signal regime, cells approach peak chemotactic alignment while absorbing a small fraction of the diffusion-limited uptake, with the required number of uptake events set by the relative concentration change across the cell. Re-analysis of Dictyostelium chemotaxis places the cell at 0.25% of that limit with a minor metabolic load, suggesting that sensing is limited by counting noise rather than by energetic cost.
