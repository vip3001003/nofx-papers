# Geometric control of diffusive relaxation: spectral design, transport bounds and transverse mode isolation

- **arXiv**: [2611.25173](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25173)
- **日期**: 2026-11-18
- **分类**: complexity
- **作者**: Pengfei Zhu
- **NOFX相关度**: 1

## 摘要

Geometric confinement changes both thermal relaxation and heat throughput, but these effects need not be governed by the same conduction path. We establish a distinction between serial and parallel geometries for passive, homogeneous Fourier conductors at fixed material properties, volume and length. For a reduced variable-area conductor, two logarithmic shape harmonics independently tune two selected Bloch-rate pairs, and exact local transport level sets permit spectral redistribution at fixed effective diffusivity. A nonperturbative weighted Poincaré bound, however, requires the effective axial diffusivity to vanish if the first positive insulated rate tends to zero at fixed length. This obstruction is avoided by continuous parallel paths with weak transverse exchange. Capacity-weighted networks identify the exchange mechanism, and a three-dimensional product domain provides an exact bulk realization. Its axial conductance remains $κV/L^2$, while exactly one positive insulated rate vanishes and the next approaches a positive limit set by axial, chamber and neck diffusion. The slow rate is asymptotically the transverse neck conductance times the sum of inverse chamber capacities. Spectral projection estimates translate this separation into a preparation- and readout-dependent observation window. The result is metastable retention of a temperature contrast under insulated conditions, not a second equilibrium state. It identifies when geometrical control of internal equilibration can be separated from macroscopic heat transport.
