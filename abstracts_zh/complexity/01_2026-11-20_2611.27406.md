# How many states does an exact relaxation model need? Sharp state complexity and geometric obstructions for reversible chains

- **arXiv**: [2611.27406](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27406)
- **日期**: 2026-11-20
- **分类**: complexity
- **作者**: Murad Aznagulov
- **NOFX相关度**: 1

## 摘要

How many states does a reversible Markov model need to reproduce two prescribed relaxation modes exactly? We maximize the smaller of the two stationary coordinate variances over all finite reversible chains whose states lie in a given convex region and whose coordinates relax at rates $1$ and $ρ$. Detailed balance turns this into the equilibrium of a tension-only cable network whose loads depend on the stationary masses. For the moment curve of the Wright-Fisher diffusion no finite chain is optimal: maximizing sequences converge to a unique beta law, and the best deficit with $N$ states is of order $N^{-2}$. We find the exact leading constant for states on the curve and for states in its convex hull, together with the optimal cube-root density of states. The hull constant is the curve constant times $c/(1+c)$, $c=(ρ-2)/ρ$; at $ρ=5/2$ the hull needs $\sqrt{6}$ times fewer states, although the limiting law is the same. For $ρ>8/3$ any upward enlargement of the hull lowers the constant again. All lower bounds hold for arbitrary transition graphs. At every fixed number of states an optimizer exists. On a seven-gon the smallest optimal chain jumps from three to four states while the optimal value stays constant, and a family of polygons forces the state count to grow linearly with the number of sides. Monotone vector fields give exact upper certificates, which solve an asymmetric quadrilateral at all rates and 381 polygon-rate instances in exact arithmetic. We formulate a finite-realizability problem and a finite-dictionary conjecture for polygons, and derive state budgets and exclusion tests for finite kinetic models.
