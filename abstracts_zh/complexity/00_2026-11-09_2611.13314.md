# Chemotactic recruitment offsets congestion in honeybee trophallaxis

- **arXiv**: [2611.13314](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.13314)
- **日期**: 2026-11-09
- **分类**: complexity
- **作者**: William Magrogan, Orit Peleg, Zachary P. Kilpatrick
- **NOFX相关度**: 0

## 摘要

Honeybee colonies distribute food by trophallaxis, a sequence of local interactions in which nectar donors transfer resources to hungry nestmates. Efficient transfer requires a searcher to find a donor and then be served. Chemical signals may draw searchers to a donor, though the crowd they form may block the way. We develop a stochastic framework coupling velocity-jump search that depends on chemical signaling to random-order service at donor aggregations, and measure trophallactic efficiency by the time the colony needs to feed a prescribed fraction of its members. Directed recruitment emerges because fed bees linger near donors and strengthen local chemical cues, but lingering also slows service. Trophallactic efficiency is therefore maximized at an intermediate abandonment rate that balances recruitment against congestion. We determine when displaced cues remain useful and how competing donors partition searchers. With two donors of unequal service rate, the faster one produces fed bees sooner and so advertises more strongly, drawing recruitment toward the donor that can sustain more throughput and away from the one that would congest. Together, these results show how feedback between service and signaling can self-organize recruitment to match local service capacity without centralized coordination.
