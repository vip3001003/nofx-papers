# Interactions from memory footprints: Cage-hopping, mnemotaxis & nonreciprocity

- **arXiv**: [2611.17257](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17257)
- **日期**: 2026-11-12
- **分类**: complexity
- **作者**: Jyoti Prasad Banerjee, Sambodh Sinha, Shashi Thutupalli et al.
- **NOFX相关度**: 0

## 摘要

Cellular motility is integral to biological processes ranging from foraging to immune responses and cancer metastasis. While cells often navigate by sensing pre-existing environmental cues, many actively steer their future trajectories by remodeling their surroundings via shared, long-lived physicochemical trails called memory footprints. These stigmergic responses to shared footprints also influence large-scale self-organization, such as the early stages of microcolony formation. Despite recent progress, a unified framework connecting single-cell dynamics and collective organization through effective pairwise interactions remains elusive. To bridge this gap, we perform extensive agent-based simulations of an interacting random walk model that explicitly accounts for footprint deposition and decay. Our approach reveals distinct cage-hopping dynamics in solitary cells, as well as complex mnemotactic responses to externally imposed memory gradients. Furthermore, we demonstrate that shared spatial memory fields generate nontrivial effective pairwise interactions, both reciprocal and nonreciprocal, that shape the collective organization of cell populations. Beyond multiscale cellular dynamics, our findings have broader implications for the collective behavior of synthetic active matter, such as swarm robots.
