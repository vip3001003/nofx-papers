# Optimal staircase structure for bacterial adaptation in antibiotic landscapes

- **arXiv**: [2611.16325](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.16325)
- **日期**: 2026-11-12
- **分类**: complexity
- **作者**: Kento Matsukawa, Shin-ichi Sasa, Namiko Mitarai
- **NOFX相关度**: 0

## 摘要

Spatial heterogeneity in antibiotic environments drives the evolution of bacterial resistance. However, how the discrete structure of these environments controls the time required for adaptation of bacteria remains unclear. We study this question using a stochastic model on a two-dimensional lattice whose axes represent physical space and phenotype space, comparing different numbers of steps while holding the overall spatial and concentration ranges fixed and scaling the migration and mutation rates by their end-to-end first-passage times. We find that increasing the number of steps accelerates the search for the next adapted state but increases the cumulative cost of repeated population saturation; this competition can produce an optimum at an intermediate number of steps. Direct Gillespie simulations reproduce the predicted dependence of the adaptation time on the number of steps, providing testable predictions for how discrete environmental structure shapes the timescale of evolutionary adaptation.
