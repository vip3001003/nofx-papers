# Mean-field theory of neural networks: from spikes to firing rates

- **arXiv**: [2611.17512](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.17512)
- **日期**: 2026-11-12
- **分类**: complexity
- **作者**: Tawan T. A. Carvalho, Matheus Haas, Denis D. Caprioti et al.
- **NOFX相关度**: 0

## 摘要

Connecting microscopic neuronal spiking to macroscopic brain function is a central challenge in computational neuroscience. Firing-rate models provide a framework for addressing this problem by describing neuronal activity at an intermediate level between individual spikes and large-scale population dynamics. Here, we introduce the foundations of firing-rate descriptions, formalizing their definitions through temporal, population, and trial averaging. We discuss experimental evidence for firing rates, from the input-output transfer functions of individual neurons to the tuning curves of different neuronal populations. We then establish the connection between spikes and firing rates in single neurons and recurrently connected excitatory-inhibitory populations using mean-field approximation. We illustrate how collective dynamics emerge from neuronal interactions, including population stimulus-response curves and bistability induced by recurrent coupling, and brain rhythms arising from synaptic depression. We briefly review extensions and applications of firing-rate models to learning, seizure-like oscillatory activity, visual selectivity, phase-amplitude coupling in neural oscillations, and whole-brain modeling. Together, these examples illustrate how firing-rate models provide effective descriptions of neural activity, linking the dynamics of individual neurons to collective computations across scales.
