# From Growth Inhibition to Fluctuations: Testing an Ising Model of Bacterial Responses to Antibiotics

- **arXiv**: [2611.31609](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31609)
- **日期**: 2026-11-23
- **分类**: complexity
- **作者**: Pietro Cicuta, Morten Kals, Nir S. Gov
- **NOFX相关度**: 0

## 摘要

Complex networks of biochemical reactions have been proposed to exhibit emergent cooperativity that can be described by an effective Ising model, with a small number of parameters encoding collective interactions. Here this mapping is experimentally tested using the response of three bacterial species to a range of antibiotics acting on different cellular targets. The Ising description is compared to the conventional Hill model, examining both the mean growth-rate response and, importantly, its population growth-rate heterogeneity (PGRH). While both models describe the mean growth-rate dependence on antibiotic concentration comparably well, we find that the Ising model provides a markedly better description of PGRH, offering an additional and more stringent test of the collective description. The fitted Ising interaction strength is systematically related to the Hill exponent and increases with the functional distance of the antibiotic target from the protein-production machinery. These results provide experimental support for describing complex biochemical reaction networks through effective cooperative Ising interactions, and suggest that the collective response of bacterial growth to antibiotics reflects the organization of the underlying metabolic network.
