# Open Quantum Time Crystals without External Thermodynamic Resources: Go and No-Go Theorems

- **arXiv**: [2611.09540](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09540)
- **日期**: 2026-11-06
- **分类**: complexity
- **作者**: Yuri Ogami, Hiroyasu Tajima
- **NOFX相关度**: 1

## 摘要

Time crystals are phases of matter that spontaneously break time-translation symmetry. They have been ruled out in the ground and Gibbs states of isolated systems with only short-range interactions, yet they have been proposed and observed experimentally in nonequilibrium settings such as Floquet and open systems. However, existing proposals for time crystals in open quantum systems require an external supply of thermodynamic resources, such as work or energetic coherence. Whether coupling to an environment, without any such resource supply, can induce a time crystal absent in the isolated system remains open. Here, we answer this question systematically. We first construct an explicit model and prove that coupling to an environment can activate a time crystal absent in the isolated system, even without such resource supply. We consider Lindblad master equations with (i) time-independent Hamiltonian and jump operators, (ii) time-translation covariance, and (iii) detailed balance---conditions that hold in standard derivations unless thermodynamic resources are supplied externally. Our model satisfies these conditions, and the activation occurs in a stationary state of both the isolated and open systems. We give several implementations of this dynamics with a zero-temperature Gibbs environment, a time-independent, energy-conserving interaction, and only short-range Hamiltonians, one of which exactly reproduces the time-crystalline periodicity. We then prove a no-go theorem: under (i)--(iii), activation can arise only from an environment-induced Hamiltonian term, not from dissipation. Our results show that time crystals can be activated in open systems without external resource supply and identify the physical requirements on the system and environment for this activation through our general no-go theorem.
