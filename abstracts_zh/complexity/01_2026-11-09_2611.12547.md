# Memory in biological Lévy walks from the dynamic-entropy formalism

- **arXiv**: [2611.12547](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12547)
- **日期**: 2026-11-09
- **分类**: complexity
- **作者**: Adriano Barra, Andrea Ladiana
- **NOFX相关度**: 1

## 摘要

Lévy walks are recognized by the histogram of their flight lengths, while the renewal hypothesis on which the textbook model rests, that every flight is drawn afresh, is seldom tested. We test it with the dynamic-entropy formalism. Maximizing the causal entropy of a sequence of logarithmic flight lengths at fixed lagged correlations yields a chain of continuous spins on a compact interval, whose depth, selected by the data, tells a renewal walker from a Markovian one and from one with a longer memory. With one lag the chain is exactly solvable. A duality that exchanges long and short flights locates a first-order condensation of the flights at the upper cutoff, with the logarithmic range of the flights in the role of system size, and a positive memory makes the histogram heavier-tailed than the rule that generates each flight. For groups, the same construction separates the memory of each individual from delayed interactions and from drives common to all units, which an analysis of the group average confounds, and it gives the collective speed a critical point. On open data, renewal is rejected in seven of ten conditions of Drosophila larvae and in every termite, whose memory reaches a few flights back and lowers the exponent of the histogram by more than half a unit below that of the one-step rule. Wild baboons align with where their nearest neighbors, those ahead above all, were heading 10 to 30 s earlier, and homing pigeons are coupled across their small flocks. Zebrafish T cells, a negative control, remember their own speed, but no delayed coupling between the speeds of different cells survives the controls.
