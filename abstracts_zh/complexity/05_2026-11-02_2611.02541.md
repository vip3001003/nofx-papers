# Topological origin of the Gutenberg-Richter law: Dynamic decoupling of fault cascades and energy dissipation

- **arXiv**: [2611.02541](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02541)
- **日期**: 2026-11-02
- **分类**: complexity
- **作者**: Bin Chen, Mingze Zhao, Puyu Cao
- **NOFX相关度**: 5

## 摘要

The Gutenberg-Richter (G-R) law is seismology's most robust empirical regularity, yet a first-principles derivation of its slope-the b-value-has remained elusive. Here, we present an analytical theory based on a finite hierarchical fault network composed of velocity-weakening asperities separated by velocity-strengthening creeping zones. By coupling network topology with fracture mechanics, we derive a generalized closed-form expression of b-value,$\(b=\frac{3}{3+2γ}\frac{\log_{10}k}{\log_{10}q}\)$, where k is the threshold branching ratio and q is the area amplification factor. Crucially, we reveal a dynamic decoupling between k and q governed by energy dissipation. The empirical universality of b=1 naturally emerges as the fault network self-organizes into a critical attractor (k=q) that maximizes two-dimensional spatial filling. Furthermore, grounded in Rate-and-State Friction rheology, we demonstrate that foreshock acceleration (inverse-Omori) and aftershock decay (Omori-Utsu) are dual manifestations of the similar hierarchical cascade. In the mean-field limit, these temporal exponents are decoupled from static fracture energy, revealing a profound orthogonality between time-dependent friction and static energy release. By unifying the G-R law, Bath's law, and the foreshock-aftershock asymmetry, this framework might provide a physics-based pathway for operational earthquake forecasting.
