# Bayesian Quantum Thermometry of Ultracold Gases with Non-Equilibrium Single-Atom Probes

- **arXiv**: [2611.27584](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27584)
- **日期**: 2026-11-20
- **分类**: complexity
- **作者**: Julian Feß, Luca Granert, Sabrina Burgardt et al.
- **NOFX相关度**: 2

## 摘要

Precise thermometry is essential for experiments with ultracold quantum gases, but conventional techniques such as time-of-flight thermometry are destructive and lose sensitivity at low atomic densities. Quantum probes provide an alternative route by encoding temperature information in accessible internal degrees of freedom. Here, we implement Bayesian thermometry with non-equilibrium spin-based single-atom probes immersed in an ultracold atomic bath. Temperature-dependent spin-exchange collisions map the bath temperature onto the internal spin populations of the probe atoms. Measurements of these populations are then used to iteratively update a Bayesian temperature estimate. We connect the information gained at different measurement settings to the underlying spin dynamics, identifying intersections of temperature-dependent population trajectories as a source of thermometric ambiguity. Using the expected information gain to optimize measurement settings, we experimentally compare unoptimized, a priori optimized, and adaptively optimized protocols. Both optimized protocols accelerate convergence, with the adaptive protocol reducing the average number of measurements required to reach a prescribed precision by a factor of 2.5 compared with the unoptimized protocol. In the many-measurement regime, we further characterize the protocols through their asymptotic error scaling and the accumulation of Fisher information, while testing consistency with the expected Cramér-Rao bound (ECRB) and the Bernstein-von Mises theorem. These results connect the microscopic dynamics of a quantum probe to efficient measurement design and establish Bayesian spin thermometry as a practical approach for ultracold-gas experiments.
