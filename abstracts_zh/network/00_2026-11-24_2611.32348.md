# Explaining intersectional segregation in multi-attribute social networks

- **arXiv**: [2611.32348](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32348)
- **日期**: 2026-11-24
- **分类**: network
- **作者**: Clara Eminente, Elisa Omodei, Michele Starnini
- **NOFX相关度**: 0

## 摘要

Segregation in students' friendships shapes their access to information and opportunities, and schools can reduce it by setting the composition of their classes. Yet segregation is usually measured one attribute at a time, although attributes such as migration background and sex are often correlated, so a preference over one can create segregation on the other. A single-attribute reading then attributes this induced segregation to the wrong preference and cannot identify which part of it a school can remove. In a network model with preferences over two correlated attributes, we derive in closed form the Coleman index, a standard segregation measure, separating the segregation due to each preference from the segregation it induces on the other attribute. In friendship networks of school classes in four European countries, ignoring sex inflates the estimated preference over migration background, increasingly so as the two attributes are more correlated. The closed form also shows what class composition can achieve. When students have no preference over one attribute, the segregation induced on it can be removed, whereas segregation set by a homophilic preference over the other cannot be changed. When both preferences are active, splitting a school with equal-sized groups and independent attributes reduces segregation only if the preferences pull in opposite directions. Judging a split by the school-wide average can hide classes that become more segregated. In the data, the split derived from the closed form removes induced segregation, and splitting does not lower segregation where both preferences are homophilic, as predicted.
