# Is price-impact decay statistically identifiable by propagator estimation? Diffusive prices force the inferred kernel to be transient and fine-tuned

- **arXiv**: [2611.10574](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.10574)
- **日期**: 2026-11-07
- **分类**: network
- **作者**: Shunta Fujiwara, Yuki Sato, Kiyoshi Kanazawa
- **NOFX相关度**: 0

## 摘要

Despite the long memory of market-order flow, price dynamics remain diffusive---a seemingly paradoxical coexistence known in econophysics as the diffusivity puzzle. The propagator model has long been regarded as a promising solution: price impact decays in time, finely balanced against the order-flow memory, and propagators inferred from data have been reported to be consistent with this fine balance. Here we prove a no-go theorem: asymptotic impact decay is not statistically identifiable by propagator estimation, since for diffusive prices the fine-tuned decay is returned whether or not the true impact decays. It rests on universal theorems for the Wiener--Hopf deconvolution by which propagators are inferred under long-memory order flow: (i) the inferred propagator is necessarily transient, even when the true impact is permanent; (ii) under a mild condition, it decays with the fine-balance exponent, fixed by the order-flow autocorrelation alone. The fine-tuned decay is thus a property of the deconvolution, not evidence of impact decay. We demonstrate this with an exactly solvable null model that retains order splitting and the square-root law but whose impact is strictly permanent: the inferred propagator shows an artificial fine-tuned decay although the true impact never decays. Nonlinear propagator estimation, being of Wiener--Hopf type, is subject to the same theorems whenever true metaorders have long memory.
