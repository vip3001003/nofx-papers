# Evolution of cooperation in enterprise resource-sharing networks with dynamic participation and adaptive decision-making

- **arXiv**: [2611.12962](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12962)
- **日期**: 2026-11-07
- **分类**: network
- **作者**: Pengfei Liu, Bin Pi, Qin Li et al.
- **NOFX相关度**: 3

## 摘要

Resource sharing among enterprises is an important mechanism for improving resource utilization efficiency and facilitating collaborative development. However, the emergence and sustainability of cooperative behavior depend critically on whether enterprises choose to participate in resource-sharing activities and how they balance cooperation and competition in their decision-making processes. To investigate these issues, we develop an evolutionary cooperation model on the enterprise resource-sharing network that integrates participation status, decision-making modes, and cooperative strategies within a unified framework. Specifically, enterprises dynamically switch between active and dormant states according to their aspiration levels, while Q-learning is employed to enable adaptive selection between payoff-driven and conformist decision-making modes. Meanwhile, interactions between resource-sharing and non-sharing in active enterprises are characterized by the snowdrift game. Through extensive simulations, we systematically examine the effects of the payoff parameter, the reinforcement-learning parameters, the participation and Q-learning mechanism, and network size on enterprise participation and the evolution of cooperation. The results show that an intermediate aspiration level is most favorable for sustaining enterprise participation and facilitating the emergence of cooperation. Moreover, the dynamic activation mechanism effectively mitigates the decline in cooperation under high cost-to-benefit ratios, allowing the system to sustain a decent level of cooperative behavior. These findings highlight the important roles of dynamic participation and adaptive decision-making in shaping the evolution of resource-sharing cooperation and provide a new modeling framework to understand the emergence and persistence of cooperation in complex enterprise networks.
