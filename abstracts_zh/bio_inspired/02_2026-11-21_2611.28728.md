# Neural-Network Approximators for Low-Thrust Multi-Asteroid Rendezvous Optimization

- **arXiv**: [2611.28728](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.28728)
- **日期**: 2026-11-21
- **分类**: bio_inspired
- **作者**: Xingyu Zhou, Roberto Armellin, Jack Yarndley et al.
- **NOFX相关度**: 2

## 摘要

Designing low-thrust multi-asteroid rendezvous missions requires the coupled selection of asteroid sequences, encounter epochs, and low-thrust trajectories. Lambert transfers support rapid beam search but do not directly describe thrust-limited transfer capacity or propellant consumption. This paper develops two complementary low-thrust neural approximators. The first directly predicts the continuous maximum admissible initial mass from numerically optimized low-thrust transfers, providing a transfer-capacity estimate for comparison with the current spacecraft mass. The second predicts mass-dependent propellant consumption and propagates spacecraft mass to subsequent legs. With the carefully-designed input features and network structure, the approximators reduce mean absolute errors by 72.2% and 75.5%, respectively, relative to conventional deep neural network baselines. These predictions are incorporated into a Lambert-ranked beam search through resource filtering and recursive mass propagation before final beam selection. The method is evaluated on fixed-visit, maximum-visit, and deployment-collection missions. Across the tested cases, the proposed resource filtering increases the fraction of candidate sequences that yield accepted low-thrust solutions, raises the maximum validated asteroid-visit count by up to 15.4%, and improves the best mission objective by up to 17.8%. These results demonstrate that continuous transfer-capacity and propellant predictions can improve finite-width sequence search.
